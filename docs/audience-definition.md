# Global Enterprise audience definition

Status: working audience definition  
Owner: Global Enterprise  
Last updated: 2026-08-24

## Purpose

Global Enterprise speaks to people who must make complex institutions more capable, governable, interoperable, and resilient. The site is not limited to buyers. It also serves the practitioners who implement the work, the people who procure or enable it, the researchers who extend it, the partners who deliver it, and the talent who may join it.

The audience model should shape navigation, service pages, insights, reports, calls to action, and careers content.

## Priority order

Priority is based on strategic fit, mandate consequence, and the audience's ability to move an institutional decision—not on how senior the title sounds.

- **P0 · Primary institutional audience:** the people who set the mandate, own the architecture, operate the change, procure the work, or deliver it.
- **P1 · Growth and relationship audience:** the partners, researchers, and talent who extend the work and make future mandates possible.
- **P2 · Amplifier audience:** capital, analysts, media, and informed readers who validate, refer, or contextualize the work.

The site should always preserve the executive route while making the practitioner, procurement, research, and talent routes explicit.

## Destination routing

Global Enterprise is the direct home for institutional advisory, enterprise architecture, ITIL/service systems, government transformation, procurement-safe context, insights, reports, and leadership conversations. The site should not broadly redirect those audiences away. It should provide the material that helps them understand the problem and then route a specific audience to the organization that owns the next experience.

Use the most specific intent first:

| Reader intent | Primary destination | Global Enterprise role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interns, apprentices, fellows, new graduates, veterans, career switchers, and professional learners | [Ignite Curiosity](https://ignitecuriosity.org/) | Explain the work and make the early-career handoff explicit |
| Anyone seeking a career with the organization, including experienced specialists | [Tao Staff](https://taostaff.com/) | Set expectations and point to the organization-wide career home |
| Professors, principal investigators, university labs, think tanks, and researchers | [INSTAR Lab](https://instarlab.org/) | Provide field-grounded context, insights, and a clear research handoff |
| Primes, subcontractors, SMEs, capture teams, and teaming partners | [DreamLimited](https://dreamlimited.org/) | Provide vendor, trust, capability, and delivery-boundary context |
| Government, enterprise, multilateral, practitioner, acquisition, analyst, and other audiences | Global Enterprise internal routes | Own the content, service pathways, reports, trust material, and leadership engagement |

The active DreamLimited destination is `dreamlimited.org`. `dreamlimited.com` currently resolves to a parked domain, so it must not be used as a public CTA until ownership and destination status change. The four external destinations are organizational handoffs, not claims of employment, partnership, endorsement, or guaranteed referral unless the relevant organization has formally approved that language.

The routing precedence is: early-career and professional learning → research and academic inquiry → prime/subcontractor teaming → other career-seeking talent → Global Enterprise default. Ambiguous or multi-audience pages stay on Global Enterprise and expose clearly labeled outbound options rather than redirecting the whole page.

## Audience roles

The same person can occupy more than one role. Site content should distinguish the role being addressed in the moment.

| Role | Typical readers | Content they need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mandate owner | President, prime minister, CEO, minister, board, agency head | Executive briefs, strategic choices, consequence, confidence, and next decision |
| Economic buyer | CIO, COO, CFO, cabinet office, agency executive, transformation sponsor | Scope, outcomes, risk, evidence, delivery model, and engagement path |
| Technical authority | Chief architect, enterprise architect, CTO, CISO, CDO, platform leader | Architecture, controls, interoperability, technical trade-offs, and transition design |
| Service and delivery operator | ITIL leader, service owner, PMO, program director, product owner, DevSecOps lead | Cadence, artifacts, decision rights, service transition, measures, and operating practice |
| Acquisition evaluator | Contracting officer, COR, procurement official, prime capture lead | Clear problem framing, relevant capability, evidence, boundaries, and procurement-safe language |
| Research and knowledge partner | Professor, lab director, think tank, standards body, analyst | Original frameworks, sources, research questions, and collaboration paths |
| Future operator | Intern, MBA/MPA student, apprentice, career switcher, experienced candidate | Role clarity, standards, examples of practice, mentorship, and careers |

## Site implications

Global Enterprise should organize pathways around reader intent rather than a flat list of job titles:

1. **Set a national or enterprise direction** — for political principals, CEOs, CIOs, and executive sponsors.
2. **Design the architecture and operating model** — for chief architects, solution architects, FEAF practitioners, and transformation leaders.
3. **Run the service and change system** — for ITIL leaders, service owners, PMOs, DevSecOps teams, and program operators.
4. **Specify or procure the work** — for contracting officers, CORs, acquisition teams, primes, and SMEs.
5. **Research, teach, and partner** — for professors, labs, standards bodies, analysts, and technology partners.
6. **Learn and join** — for interns, MBA/MPA/MPP students, technical learners, experienced specialists, and career switchers.

## Boundary

The site is intentionally not positioned as a general-purpose IT outsourcing, staffing, consumer technology, or entry-level tutorial site. It can educate broadly, but its center of gravity is institutional consequence: governments, global enterprises, multilateral systems, the practitioners who operate them, and the people who want to become capable of doing this work.
