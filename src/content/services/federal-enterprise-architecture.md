Federal architecture work is most useful when it helps an accountable team make a decision: what capability to fund, which dependency to remove, what service to protect, or what transition to sequence next. A framework name can provide shared vocabulary, but the practice has to connect mission, people, information, technology, controls, and investment in the environment where the work will actually run.

This is a consultative practice informed by FEAF concepts and federal architecture vocabulary. It is not FEAF certification, an agency designation, or a substitute for an agency's policy, acquisition, security, privacy, budget, or authorization authorities.

## Reader job: trace the mandate into a governable architecture

This route is for federal CIO, CTO, CDO, CAIO, CISO, mission, portfolio, and architecture teams that need a working set from mandate to transition. The immediate job is to make the current decision legible:

- What public or mission outcome is the institution protecting?
- Which capabilities and services carry that outcome?
- Where do information, applications, platforms, people, suppliers, and controls create dependencies?
- Which investment or transition choice can be tested now, and what evidence would change it?

The result is a decision-ready architecture conversation, not a static target-state presentation.

## A FEAF-informed method that stays close to the work

We begin with the mandate and the service a person or mission team has to experience. From there, the practice can organize views around mission and capability, information and data, applications and services, technology and platforms, security and control questions, and investment and transition. The exact views follow the decision; they are not a claim that every organization should reproduce one template.

The working sequence is:

1. **Frame the mandate.** Name the outcome, service promise, constraints, authorities, and assumptions that govern the question.
2. **Map the capability.** Connect mission activities to the people, information, services, platforms, suppliers, and measures that make them possible.
3. **Expose the boundary.** Record interfaces, ownership, data stewardship, dependencies, risks, and the decisions that require a design authority or executive forum.
4. **Compare the transition.** Describe current, target, and interim states, then select a small move that creates evidence without hiding continuity risk.
5. **Tie architecture to investment.** Make the relationship between capability need, portfolio choice, funding gate, delivery increment, and expected evidence explicit.
6. **Set the review rhythm.** Give each artifact a steward, trigger, decision owner, and route for updating the architecture when the mission or evidence changes.

## The federal architecture working set

An engagement can produce a maintained set of artifacts such as:

- a **mandate-to-capability map** connecting mission intent to services, outcomes, and ownership;
- a **current, target, and transition architecture** showing the interfaces and dependencies that matter to the decision;
- an **information and data responsibility view** identifying authoritative sources, stewardship, access questions, and evidence gaps;
- an **investment traceability register** linking capability needs to portfolio choices, assumptions, and review gates;
- a **decision, risk, and evidence log** that records what is known, what is inferred, and what still needs validation;
- a **governance and architecture review calendar** that makes exceptions and change triggers visible;
- a **service transition and capability-transfer note** showing who will operate the new default and how continuity will be checked.

## Practice boundary and evidence

We can help a team prepare architecture views, investment traceability, decision records, and transition questions. We do not issue FEAF credentials or certify an architecture. We do not grant, approve, or stand in for an authorization decision, an ATO, or an RMF authority. When security or authorization work is relevant, the practice identifies the questions, evidence owners, and handoffs that the authorized agency roles must resolve.

The public site also does not claim federal clients, agency endorsement, contract-vehicle access, or mission authority. Named institutions may be relevant context for a reader without being a client, partner, member, or source of endorsement. Engagement-specific evidence, roles, classification, and delivery conditions belong in the appropriate diligence channel.
